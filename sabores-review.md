# Sabores Mexican Cuisine — Website & Digital Growth Review

**Reviewed:** August 16, 2026 · **Site:** http://sabores.ca · **Reviewed by:** Hermes (business-growth lens)
**Method:** Live fetch of sabores.ca (raw HTML + rendered browser view of every section), SkipTheDishes listing, Calgary Farmers' Market vendor pages, TripAdvisor, Yelp, and directory listings.

---

## Snapshot

Sabores is a well-liked, authentic Mexican food operation in Calgary with two physical presences:

- **Calgary Farmers' Market West** — 25 Greenbriar Dr NW (sit-down + takeout; Wed–Sun, market hours)
- **Cedarbrae / production kitchen** — 11237 30 St SW (wholesale kitchen by appointment; takeout/delivery Fri–Sun)

The **food reputation is strong** (TripAdvisor 4.6/5; reviewers call the salsas and tamales the best they've found in Calgary), and they already have a **live SkipTheDishes ordering page with a full, priced menu**. The problem is that **the website itself is a near-broken asset**: it is an unfinished-looking GoDaddy template that does none of the selling, and the digital footprint around it is fragmented and inconsistent.

---

## 1) What the site does well

Honestly: very little, and it's important to say that plainly.

- **The brand name and core message exist.** "Sabores" = flavors, and the tagline about authentic Mexican tradition is present and on-message.
- **Two locations and their hours are listed** (in the Locate Us section), including the distinction between the market restaurant and the production kitchen.
- **Social links exist** (Facebook, Instagram) — though they point to the *wrong, smaller* Instagram account (see below).
- **A contact form exists**, so a customer can reach out without hunting for an email address.

That is the complete list. Everything else is either missing, broken, or actively hurting the business.

---

## 2) What is missing or weak

### Critical (hurting the business now)

1. **The site is an untouched GoDaddy starter template.** Its search-engine description literally reads *"Check out this GoDaddy hosted webpage! http://sabores.ca."* That is the text Google shows under the restaurant's name in search results. It tells a potential customer the site is unfinished and abandoned. This alone suppresses trust and clicks.

2. **"Our Food" is a dead link.** Clicking it shows nothing. For a restaurant, the menu page being empty is the single most damaging failure on the site.

3. **No menu anywhere on the site.** The full, priced menu exists on SkipTheDishes (tacos, tamales, enchiladas, burritos, pozole, chilaquiles, 11 house-made salsas, tres leches — all at fair prices). None of it is on the website. Customers can't browse what they'd be eating.

4. **No online ordering link, no delivery link, no reservation or "order ahead" path.** The site has zero conversion actions. The search for "order," "menu," "delivery," "takeout" across the whole rendered page returns nothing.

5. **No phone number, no address clickable, no map, no "Get Directions."** The Locate Us section is plain text only — no tel: link, no map embed, no directions button. On mobile (where most local searches happen) that's a lost customer.

### Significant (fragmenting trust and discovery)

6. **Fixed desktop layout.** The page forces `width=1024` on phones. It renders badly on mobile — and mobile is where restaurant lookups happen.

7. **The site links to the wrong Instagram account.** The site's Locate Us links to @saboresmexicancuisine (520 followers, 68 posts), while the Calgary Farmers' Market vendor page links to @saboresyyc (938 followers, 62 posts). **Two separate accounts, both active, split one audience.** This dilutes followers and engagement and looks careless to anyone who notices.

8. **Inconsistent phone numbers and hours across directories.**
   - Site: no phone at all.
   - Yelp / Restaurant Guru: (587) 352-9099; hours Thu–Sun 11am–8pm.
   - Loc8NearMe: (825) 488-9098; hours Wed–Sun 9am–7pm.
   - Site hours (Wed–Sun 9–7) don't match Yelp hours (Thu–Sun 11–8).
   - A customer researching the restaurant sees conflicting facts. In local search, this inconsistency also erodes Google ranking.

9. **2000s-era relics still on the page.** The share buttons include **MySpace, Del.icio.us, and StumbleUpon** — dead platforms from 15 years ago. This is visual proof the site hasn't been touched in a decade or more.

10. **No story, no photos of the food, no owner/chef voice.** The About Us section is a generic Wikipedia-style history of Mexican cuisine (Mayans, Aztecs, Spanish colonials). Nothing about *this* restaurant, its people, or its food. The "Our Food" section that could hold photos is dead. Food photos are the #1 appetite driver for restaurants — the site has effectively none.

### Minor

11. **Thin review count.** 4.6/5 is excellent, but only ~17 reviews across platforms. For a restaurant this well-liked, the review volume is far below what the quality deserves — meaning most happy customers never get asked to leave one.

12. **No email capture, no loyalty, no events, no news.** Nothing to bring a visitor back.

## 3) Prioritized growth opportunities

Ranked by **impact ÷ effort**. All are realistic for a small owner-operated restaurant.

### #1 — Fix the Google Business Profile and make every directory say the same thing (Highest ROI, ~1–2 days)
- Pick **one canonical phone number, one address, one set of hours** and update it everywhere: Google Business Profile, Yelp, TripAdvisor, SkipTheDishes, the Farmers' Market vendor page, and the website.
- Claim/verify the Google listing if not already done; add the real menu, food photos, and the correct hours (Wed–Sun, closed Mon–Tue must be prominent).
- **Why it matters:** Local search is how Calgary finds restaurants. One inconsistent phone number or hour costs ranking and trust. This is the cheapest, most leveraged fix available.
- **Ask happy customers for Google reviews** — a 4.6 with 17 reviews can become a 4.6 with 100+ in a few months of simply asking (QR code at the counter / after delivery).

### #2 — Replace the template site with a simple mobile-first one-pager that converts (High ROI, 1–2 weeks)
- A modern one-page site (or even a Google Business Profile-linked free site) with: a hero food photo, the tagline, **a real menu with prices and photos**, the two locations with **tap-to-call and Get Directions buttons**, hours, and **one big "Order Online" button** pointing to their existing SkipTheDishes page.
- Minimum bar: delete the default meta description, remove the MySpace/Del.icio.us/StumbleUpon buttons, and fix or remove the dead "Our Food" link.
- **Why it matters:** the website is the first thing a skeptical customer checks after search results. Right now it says "we don't care about this." A clean one-pager says "we're serious."
- Cost can be near-zero (Google Business Profile site or a simple builder); a paid option (Square/Wix/custom) is a small one-time expense.

### #3 — Make "Order Online" a single obvious action everywhere (High ROI, days)
- They already run SkipTheDishes with a complete, well-priced menu. The website simply never tells anyone.
- Add a persistent **"Order Online"** button to: the top of the website, the Facebook page, both Instagram bios (link in bio), and the Farmers' Market vendor profile.
- Consider also enabling **order-ahead/pickup** at the market stall (market shoppers already walk by; let them skip the line).
- **Why it matters:** every one of these links is a free funnel to a transaction they've already built the plumbing for.

### #4 — Consolidate to ONE Instagram account and feed it consistently (Medium ROI, ongoing)
- Merge @saboresyyc (938 followers) and @saboresmexicancuisine (520 followers) into one handle — keep the larger, link the smaller, update the website and all listings to the winner.
- Post 3–4×/week: food close-ups (tamales, tres leches, the 11 salsas), the market booth, weekly specials, and short behind-the-counter clips. These are inherently photogenic and reviewers already rave about them.
- **Why it matters:** a single, active account compounds: local hashtags (#yyc #yycfood #calgaryfarmersmarket), location tags, and check-ins feed both discovery and review growth. Two split accounts do half the work each.

### #5 — Turn the Farmers' Market presence into repeat business (Medium ROI, ongoing)
- They already have the best foot-traffic asset in Calgary — the market. Add a small **email/SMS list** at the booth ("get the weekly special + secret salsa drop"), a punch-card or loyalty perk, and post market-exclusive items (their tres leches and seasonal salsas are perfect hooks).
- Coordinate with the market's event calendar and cross-post from the market's own channels (the market already has a vendor page and socials for them).
- **Why it matters:** market customers are warm, local, and repeatable. Capturing them directly converts the market's foot traffic into a relationship the restaurant owns — not one rented from a delivery app.

---

## Bottom line

The food and operations are ahead of the digital presence. The website is a decade-old template doing active harm; the ordering channel exists but is invisible; and the local-search footprint is inconsistent. The single most valuable week of work is: **canonicalize the business info across Google/directories, replace the template site with a mobile one-pager that has a real menu + Order Online, and ask happy customers for reviews.** Everything else compounds from there.
